\begin{abstract}
AgriQ integrates machine learning and quantum-inspired optimization to recommend
optimal crop allocations across agricultural parcels. A Random Forest classifier
evaluates crop suitability from soil and climate features, an LSTM forecasts
commodity prices, and a QUBO formulation is solved via Simulated Annealing and
Simulated Bifurcation to maximize expected profit under demand constraints.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Smart crop planning requires combining agronomic knowledge, market forecasts, and
 combinatorial optimization. AgriQ addresses this for Syrian agriculture by
 linking open datasets (Kaggle, FAO, ARIJ) with NASA POWER weather data.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Data Preparation}
Parcels are generated across five governorates. Weather is fetched from NASA POWER.
EcoCrop-style thresholds produce a suitability matrix.

\subsection{Machine Learning}
A Random Forest classifies crop suitability from $(N, P, K, T, H, \text{pH}, R)$
 features. An LSTM forecasts FAO price time series.

\subsection{Optimization}
The allocation problem is formulated as a QUBO:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{x}} \; \mathbf{x}^T Q \mathbf{x}
\end{equation}
where $x_{ij} = 1$ if parcel $i$ is assigned crop $j$. Penalty terms enforce
one-crop-per-parcel and demand caps. We compare Simulated Annealing (SA) and
Simulated Bifurcation (SB).
